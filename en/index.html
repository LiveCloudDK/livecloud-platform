Why we're different

The user is the centre. Everything else hangs off it.

The ticket, the payment, the actions and what the guest gets to see sit on the same profile, tied to a real person through verified ID. That is what holds the platform together, and it is the one thing a stack of separate suppliers cannot do.

The ticket

It lives on the profile, not as a PDF. That is why a pass can be split, a single day handed over during the festival, and the ticket resold inside your own app.

The payment

The Beats balance follows the guest, not the wristband. Every sale at the bar belongs to a person, so you know what was bought, not just how much went through the till.

The actions

Check-in at the gate, table booking, pre-order and handing a ticket to a friend. All of it on the same account, leaving the same trail.

The personalisation

Because the system knows the three above, it can show each guest something that fits where they are and what they have done. Not the same message to the whole festival.

Whoever scanned the ticket at 2pm is the same person who bought three beers at 7pm and still has Beats left on Sunday night. It sounds obvious. It is not, when the ticketing system, the payment terminal and the app come from three different suppliers.

The sum

The price should be held up against what you pay today.

A festival currently pays for the same things across four or five different suppliers. It is rarely added up in one place, and that is where the conversation about price should start.

It is the same device. The terminal that takes payment at the bar is the one that scans the ticket at the gate. Today the festival rents two kinds of hardware from two suppliers. With us, both are included in the price.

On top of that, costs drop away on their own once ticketing, payment and sales sit in the same system: the transaction fee charged twice, the cash handling, and the weeks the finance team spends making the numbers add up.

We don't know your figures. But we can work through them together in a meeting, and that exercise is what decides whether this makes sense for you.

What you stop paying for
  • Renting payment terminals
  • Renting scanners and check-in equipment
  • Transaction fees charged twice
  • Wristbands and chips from a third party
  • Cash handling and secure transport
  • Manual reconciliation and bookkeeping hours
Who we are

The people who built the system are the people you meet on the ground.

The platform is our own, from core to API. There is no third-party stack underneath and no supplier to call when something needs fixing. It also means there is nobody else to point at if it doesn't work.

There is no distance between the people who build it and the people who run it. Ringsted Festival 2026 ran on LiveCloud from the first ticket to the final settlement, and we were on the ground the whole way through. What broke on Friday night was fixed by Saturday morning. Not in a support ticket three weeks later.

We are a small team, and that is deliberate. There is no account manager between you and the people writing the code. Call us and you talk to someone who knows what is happening inside the system.

We are independent. LiveCloud is a Danish company that exists for one purpose: to build this one festival platform. No private equity fund and no outside investor needs a return out of your guests. It means we can agree to a change on Friday and have it live on Monday, and that our only agenda is that the platform works for you.

Our office is in Ringsted, the same town as the festival that the platform ran from end to end. It is ten minutes from our desks to the festival, and it shows in how fast things get fixed.

The company was founded in 2025. We are new, and we have already run a whole festival on the platform. In return we say no to more festivals than we can serve properly. We would rather be the right choice for a few than a risk to many.
