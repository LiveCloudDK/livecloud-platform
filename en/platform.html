The platform

One platform, three worlds.

For crew & B2B

Order holders get a web portal without the MitID requirement and can distribute wristbands to crew, artists, press and staff.

  • Spending cap per wristband

  • Invoice billing available

The queue

Under three seconds per transaction.

The critical half hour is the break between two sets. That is when a festival either sells or loses the sale, and it comes down to how fast the bar can serve.

Pre-ordering with dedicated fast lanes. Guests order food and drink in the app and collect at the stall in a lane of their own. The order is paid for before they arrive, so serving it is a handover rather than a transaction.

Scanning a chip wristband takes under three seconds. No card, no PIN, no round trip through the bank for every purchase. The card fee is paid once when the balance is topped up, instead of on every single beer.

Automatic top-up removes the lost sale. No declined cards and no guests running out mid-order. The balance is filled before it empties.

Guests keep their full purchase history. Every purchase, receipt and balance sits on the personal profile in the app. Nobody has to guess what they have spent, and nobody has to ask at the Service Desk.

Security and crew

Everyone on site is who they say they are.

Accreditation, access control and crew management run in the same system as the guests. That is what stops a wristband from simply being passed on.

One accreditation module for every group. Crew, external guests, press and artists sit in the same place, each with their own access level. Festival management assigns backstage, VIP, the production office or the press zone in a few clicks, straight onto the person's chip wristband.

Identity is locked with electronic ID. Because accreditation talks to the app's verification, every crew and artist profile is tied to the real person. That removes the problem of passes and wristbands being lent to someone else.

Capacity is visible in real time. Gates at the entrance and RFID portals at the exit give a live count of exactly how many people are on site. Safety officers can watch a bottleneck build before it becomes dangerous, instead of guessing from a camera feed.

Crew meals sit on the profile, not on paper. Staff Beats go straight onto their profile and are spent on the bars' own terminals. No more paper vouchers, meal tickets and shrinkage at the stalls.

Shifts are checked in on the ground. On the terminals already standing in the bar, through the team leader's app, or by geofencing. It removes the need for a large central crew office. If a volunteer misses a shift, the profile and wristband are blocked automatically, with no case for anyone to handle.
