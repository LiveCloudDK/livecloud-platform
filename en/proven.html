Adaptability

The festival doesn't stop because a cell tower goes down.

You can't control the surroundings. Cell towers fail, networks drop, and peak load never behaves as planned. That's why the platform is built with plan A, B and C, and the plans overlap, so there's always a safety net under operations, sales and safety.

If a cell tower goes down mid-event, sales continue. The POS units keep running offline, transactions sync once the connection returns, and guests notice nothing. Operations are back on track before most people realise anything was wrong.

If gates are opened without access control, for instance to ease pressure at peak, the access point automatically moves from the gate to the first purchase at a stand. The overview doesn't disappear, it switches source. We still know how many people are on the grounds and how the flow is moving.

And behind the systems stands a team working on the grounds throughout the event. When reality changes, we adapt with it. That's not a contingency plan, that's how the platform is designed.

What that means
  • Sales and safety always in place, even when infrastructure fails
  • Overlapping plans, one safety net, no single point of failure
  • Always the best possible picture of how many people are on site
  • Operations back on track without guests noticing

Ringsted Festival 2026,
in the 125-year-old Lystanlæg park.

Since 2005, Ringsted Festival has been a warm, festive meeting place for every age and every walk of life. From 30 July to 1 August 2026, the Lystanlæg was lit up by thousands of coloured bulbs along the paths and around the lakes.

Hand-built wooden cabins with dancing on the tables, food trucks with cuisines from across the world, a wealth of bars, and quirky oases with swing tables, deck chairs and lounge corners under the trees.

The platform sat underneath it all, completely invisible, and made room for what the festival is. Tickets, access, Beats, sales and accounting ran on LiveCloud from the first ticket to the final settlement.
